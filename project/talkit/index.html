02 · User Research

After surveying 130+ learners, majority often couldn’t remember a vocabulary word in a real environment.

79.1% said language learning improved with a real-world object.

talkIT explored that object and vocabulary relationship in real-time.

In person

Conducting in-person interviews, I observed and received feedback to change three things.

04 · Accessibility

Speak when it feels right.
Type when it doesn’t.

After talking with users, I found people were hesitant to use speech mode in public spaces. So I added a Speak ↔ Type toggle — learners can keep conversing in any environment, without needing to talk out loud.

05 · Final Design

Turn anything into your language teacher.

talkIT is a camera-first language app: point at something nearby, meet it as your teacher, and keep talking — Speak or Type — about what’s right in front of you.

07 · Outcome

Object-led conversations held people
three times longer than flashcards.

3×

longer than flashcards

FlashcardsStatic drills. Felt like a test.

Object-ledA chat about what’s on the table.

The camera scan became the first screen.

What I learned

Situated learning changed how I wireframe. The camera scan had to be the first screen, not a gimmick. Next time I’d instrument session length per object type during the tests, not after — I knew 3× in hindsight. And I’d track the age mix as a panel from day one: that spread was the finding, and it should have been an audit trail.
